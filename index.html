The Need

Health care moved online.
Not everyone was shown how.

Results, refills, bills, and messages now live in portals and electronic records. Most people are expected to figure them out alone, and the first try usually happens with their real health information on the line.

Patients are expected to self-serve

Portals ask people to find results, request refills, and pay bills on their own. Many older adults were never shown how, and some avoid portals altogether.

No safe place to practice

In a real portal, every click touches real records. Fear of pressing the wrong button keeps people from trying, and from asking for help.

Families help without training

Half of portal users now help someone else, often an aging parent or spouse. They learn the system on the fly, usually in the middle of a real health problem.

How It Works

Real screens. Made-up records.
Step-by-step guidance.

Tracing Care recreates the tools people meet in real health care, then adds the support a real system never gives you: hints, plain language, and room to make mistakes.

Live

Guided Practice Mode

A helper panel gives one task at a time, explains why it matters, and shows where to click if you get stuck. Finish all six tasks to print a certificate of practice.

  • Find test results and read flagged values
  • Message the care team and request refills
  • Confirm appointments and pay a bill
  • Add a family member as a helper

Realistic Patient Portal

Robin Merriweather's portal has 15 sections that mirror what people see in real portals, from vital signs and immunizations to an emergency card.

  • Test results, vitals, and visit history
  • Medications, refills, and referrals
  • Billing, documents, and care team
  • Works on computers, tablets, and phones

Our Approach

Confidence comes from practice.
Not from a manual.

Most people learn health technology the hard way: alone, in a hurry, with their own records on the screen. Tracing Care gives them a calm place to learn first, so the real portal, the real results, and the real bill feel familiar.

Patients have a right to see their health information. Knowing how to use it is what makes that right real.

01

Safe to make mistakes

Nothing in Tracing Care is real, so learners can click, explore, and get it wrong without consequences. That's how confidence starts.

02

Plain language first

Instructions use everyday words. Medical terms get explained, not assumed.

03

Teach finding, not following

Hints point to the menu first, then the button, so learners build skills they can use in any portal.

04

Built for every age

Bigger text, large buttons, and phone-friendly screens. Older adults are the first audience, not an afterthought.

05

Real-world habits

Each task ends with a practical tip, like never paying a bill through a link in a text message.

06

Honest about what it is

Tracing Care is a practice environment. It is not a medical record, and its certificate of practice is not a professional certification.

Sources & References

All statistics and claims on this page are supported by peer-reviewed research, government data, or credible institutional sources. Citations are provided for full transparency.

Government · ASTP / HHS 2025
01: Portals Are Now the Norm
Individuals' Access and Use of Patient Portals and Smartphone Health Apps, 2024
Richwine C. Office of the Assistant Secretary for Technology Policy (ASTP).
ASTP Data Brief No. 77. July 2025.

National survey data showing 77% of individuals were offered online access to their medical records in 2024 and 65% accessed a portal at least once. 59% had more than one portal, and 51% used proxy access to help someone else.

healthit.gov, ASTP Data Brief No. 77
Institutional Survey · University of Michigan 2023
02: Older Adults and Patient Portals
Use and Experiences with Patient Portals Among Older Adults
Anthony D, Singer D, Kirch M, Solway E, Roberts S, Smith E, Hutchens L, Malani P, Kullgren J.
National Poll on Healthy Aging. May 2023. 2,563 adults aged 50 to 80.

Found 78% of adults 50 to 80 have a patient portal, but 35% of non-users were not confident they could log in and navigate one. About half of portal users had given someone else access to help them.

ihpi.umich.edu, National Poll on Healthy Aging
Government · NCES 2006
03: Health Literacy by Age
The Health Literacy of America's Adults: Results From the 2003 National Assessment of Adult Literacy
Kutner M, Greenberg E, Jin Y, Paulsen C.
U.S. Department of Education, NCES 2006-483.

The most recent national measure of health literacy. Only 12% of adults scored proficient. Among adults 65 and older, 59% scored basic or below basic and only 3% were proficient.

nces.ed.gov/pubs2006/2006483.pdf
Government · HHS / ONC
04: Patients' Right to Their Data
Information Blocking Rule, 21st Century Cures Act (45 CFR Part 171)
U.S. Department of Health & Human Services, Office of the National Coordinator for Health IT.
Enforced April 5, 2021. Updated 2024.

Federal law requiring that patients can access their electronic health information without blocking. The right exists, but people still need the skills to use it, which is the gap Tracing Care helps close.

healthit.gov/topic/information-blocking
