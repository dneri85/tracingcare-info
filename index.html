The Need

Health care moved online. Not everyone was shown how.

Results, refills, bills, and messages now live in portals and electronic health records. Yet patients, families, and future health workers rarely get a safe place to learn them, and real records are too sensitive to practice on.

Patients are expected to self-serve

Portals ask people to find results, request refills, and pay bills on their own. Many older adults were never shown how, and some avoid portals altogether.

Real records, real risk

Scams targeting older adults are rising fast. Real health records are private and valuable, so families and community programs are careful about who sees them. That makes it hard to teach on a real portal.

Students can't practice on real systems

Health jobs ask for EHR experience, but training on major systems like Epic usually requires a hospital sponsor. Many students finish school without ever practicing on one.

Families help without training

About half of people who use a portal have also used one for someone else, often an aging parent or spouse. They learn the system on the fly, usually in the middle of a real health problem.

How It Works

Real screens. Made-up records. Step-by-step guidance.

Tracing Care recreates the tools people meet in real health care, then adds the support a real system never gives you: hints, plain language, and room to make mistakes.

Live

Guided Practice

One instruction at a time, told as a short story about Robin: "Robin has a visit coming up. Can you find the date and time?" Learners find answers and do real portal tasks, with supportive feedback and hints when they need them.

  • Seven everyday tasks, from visits to bills
  • "Show me where" hints and gentle feedback
  • Comfort check-in at the start and the end
  • Simple "You did it!" summary, no names needed
Live

Explore Mode

Robin Merriweather's full practice portal with no instructions, organized the way many clinic portals are, with familiar names like Visits, Test Results, Messages, and Billing Summary. Learners click around freely, just like in a real portal.

  • Home feed with shortcuts and reminders
  • My Record: test results, medications, health summary
  • Visits, messages, sharing, and billing summary
  • Works on computers, tablets, and phones

Our Approach

Confidence comes from practice. Not from a manual.

Most people learn health technology the hard way. Patients figure it out alone with their own records on the screen. Students often meet their first EHR on the job. Tracing Care gives both a calm place to learn first, using made-up but realistic information, so no one's private health data is ever at risk.

Patients have a right to see their health information. Knowing how to use it is what makes that right real.

01

Safe to make mistakes

Nothing in Tracing Care is real, so learners can click, explore, and get it wrong without consequences. That's how confidence starts.

02

Plain language first

Instructions use everyday words. Medical terms get explained, not assumed.

03

Teach finding, not following

Hints point to the menu first, then the button, so learners build skills they can use in any portal.

04

Accessible from the start

Accessibility shapes every screen from the first sketch: bigger text, strong contrast, large buttons, and phone-friendly layouts. Older adults are the first audience, not an afterthought.

05

Real-world habits

Each task ends with a practical tip, like never paying a bill through a link in a text message.

06

Honest about what it is

Tracing Care is a practice environment. It is not a medical record, and finishing practice is not a professional certification.

✓ Complete

Brand & Design

Visual identity, logo, color palette, and a clear, phone-friendly design system, with readable type and strong contrast built in.

⬤ Built In, Ongoing

Accessibility in Every Step

Part of every design decision from the start, guided by WCAG 2.2: readable text, strong color contrast, large buttons, and keyboard-friendly controls. Each new screen is checked as it's built, including with a screen reader.

Anonymous progress tracking is built in: tasks finished, help needed, and comfort before and after. Next: first sessions with older adult learners.

Adults 60 and older filed 201,266 complaints and reported $7.748 billion in losses in 2025, up 37% and 59% from 2024. Phishing and spoofing was the most reported scam (48,064 complaints), followed by tech support scams.

ic3.gov, 2025 IC3 Annual Report
